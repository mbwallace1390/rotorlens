How do I get the log off my helicopter?
  1. Connect the flight controller to this device by USB.
  2. Open the Rotorflight configurator or tuning app and connect.
  3. Go to the Blackbox tab and tap Activate mass storage device mode.
  4. The controller reappears as a drive, and this phone offers to open RotorLens. Pick the .bbl file from it.
  5. Before unplugging, turn mass storage mode off again in the configurator, or eject the drive from your phone's storage notification. Pulling the cable while it is still mounted is what makes Android warn that a device was removed unsafely. The log you opened is unaffected, and the phone does not need restarting.

On a phone you can also copy the file to local storage first, or share it straight into RotorLens. If your controller logs to an SD card, reading the card directly works too and skips the steps above.

Help RotorLens get better at this?


    
RotorLens can send the measurements from this flight — about 4 kB of numbers
      — to help work out what normal looks like across many helicopters. It is how the
      thresholds this app judges your aircraft against stop being guesses.


    
Your log never leaves your phone. What would be sent is the same handful of
      numbers the app already keeps: your gains, how well each axis tracked, head speed, and
      how much vibration was measured. Never the log, never your location, never a date, never
      a file name, and never your helicopter’s name.


    
You can see everything that has been sent, and delete all of it, at any time.


    
    
Nothing is sent yet, and this version cannot send.
      RotorLens has no INTERNET or sensitive platform permission. Saying yes records your choice and
      creates a random local identity for each saved helicopter; nothing is sent.

        What this means
        

          
    
            
  • You keep ownership. Sharing does not transfer it. Your measurements
              remain yours to use for anything.
    • 
            
  • You grant a non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free licence to use the
              measurements you share.
    • 
            
  • The purpose is derived statistics — averages, noise floors and
              thresholds computed across many helicopters. Those numbers, rather than your
              individual records, are what goes back into the app.
    • 
            
  • This includes commercial use. RotorLens is open source, and open-source
              releases may still be distributed for a fee. Improvements built on these numbers
              may ship in those releases. Saying so here rather than letting you find out later
              is the point of this section.
    • 
            
  • Your individual records are not published or passed to anyone else.
              Only the derived statistics appear in the app. Publishing the collection, or
              sharing it with the Rotorflight project, would be a different question and would
              have to be asked separately.
    • 
            
  • Deleting your shared measurements removes your records from the
              collection. Statistics already calculated from them — averages and
              thresholds across many helicopters — cannot be recalculated to exclude
              records that no longer exist, and remain in the app.
    • 
            
  • This covers these measurements and nothing else. If RotorLens ever wants
              to send anything beyond them, that is a new question rather than something
              covered by this one.
